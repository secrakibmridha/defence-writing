\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Motivation}
Bangladesh's road network expansion relies heavily on bitumen for flexible pavements, yet quality variations between local and imported sources profoundly affect long-term performance. Imported low-cost bitumen, often sourced from Iran, has frequently faced bans by agencies such as the Roads and Highways Department (RHD) due to rapid pavement deterioration and early rutting. In contrast, Eastern Refinery's laboratory-grade bitumen maintains a stable pricing structure at approximately Tk6,300 per unit while offering superior stability. Despite this, a comprehensive empirical evaluation comparing these variants under Bangladesh's subtropical climate is essential to optimize construction costs without compromising safety and durability. Furthermore, the structural integrity of the pavement is significantly dictated by aggregate properties. Incorporating varying shapes of coarse aggregates, specifically replacing conventional aggregates with a proportion of cubical aggregates, has been shown to improve the mechanical response of bituminous mixtures, necessitating an integrated study of both bitumen source and aggregate shape.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
Existing studies generally focus on fundamental bitumen properties or waste modifiers, but lack direct, localized comparisons between Eastern Refinery laboratory-grade bitumen and imported low-cost bitumen. Moreover, no comprehensive analysis integrates economic factors with essential performance metrics like load-bearing capacity and climate resistance in high-traffic areas. Additionally, while the importance of aggregate shape is acknowledged, systematic investigations analyzing the synergistic effects of locally available bitumen and specifically proportioned cubical aggregates on pavement performance remain scarce. This gap hinders policy-making for sustainable, cost-effective, and durable road construction practices in Bangladesh.

\subsection{Research Objectives}
To assess and compare the performance and cost-effectiveness of local laboratory bitumen versus imported low-cost bitumen for Bangladesh road applications, focusing on the influence of aggregate shape. The specific objectives include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To determine and compare the fundamental properties (penetration, ductility, viscosity, specific gravity, and softening point) of laboratory bitumen and imported bitumen.
    \item To investigate the effect of substituting 20\% of the coarse aggregate fraction (19 mm to 12.5 mm) with cubical aggregates on the structural integrity of the bituminous mix.
    \item To evaluate pavement performance metrics, including Marshall stability, flow, indirect tensile strength (ITS), and climate resistance (modified Marshall immersion).
    \item To perform a cost-benefit analysis integrating initial material costs with projected maintenance economics to determine long-term viability.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Significance of the Study}
Optimizing bitumen selection combined with strategic aggregate shape modifications can significantly reduce maintenance costs and enhance the road economy in Bangladesh, where infrastructure supports rapid urbanization. Superior local bitumen paired with optimal mix designs improves pavement lifespan, minimizing disruptions and the environmental impact of frequent repairs. The results of this study will inform RHD specifications, promote self-reliance in construction materials, and guide future sustainable infrastructure development.
